01 · Who we are

Igbo heritage,
cut in the Italian way.

UGO SARTORIA makes menswear for the Igbo man and for anyone who admires how he dresses: suits, senator outfits, traditional ceremonial attire and corporate wear, all cut with the discipline of Italian tailoring. We sell in euros, from Italy, to Europe, the United Kingdom and Nigeria.

The name. Ugo is the Igbo word for eagle, a symbol of excellence and honour. Sartoria is Italian for a tailoring house. The name is the brand: Igbo pride, made with Italian craft.

What we believe

  • Heritage is worn, not performed. Isiagu, senator and ceremonial dress are living clothes. We tailor them to fit as precisely as a Milanese suit, and we never turn them into costume.
  • Fit is respect. Clean shoulders, tapered sleeves, trousers with a high, comfortable rise. A garment that fits honours the man wearing it.
  • Fewer, better. We make small runs, open pre-orders before we produce, and only make what our customers have asked for.
  • Honest numbers. We publish a delivery promise and keep it. We only work with suppliers whose quotes meet our guardrails (page 10).

Who we dress

The man who wears a senator on Friday, a double-breasted suit on Monday and isiagu with a red cap at his family's ceremony, and who expects each to be made with the same care. He lives in Lagos, Enugu, London, Milan or Rome, and he buys fewer clothes of higher quality.

02 · Logo

The eagle feather.

A single eagle feather drawn in one fine line, above the wordmark UGO SARTORIA set in a high-contrast serif with wide letter spacing. The feather is the ugo, the eagle, in our name. Among the Igbo the eagle feather marks honour and achievement; in our logo it is the tailor's quill.

Use

  • Black (Ink) on Ivory is the primary version. Ivory on Ink is the reverse for dark backgrounds and the cover.
  • Keep clear space around the logo equal to the height of the letter U on every side.
  • The feather can stand alone as a mark (labels, buttons, social avatars). The wordmark never appears without the correct spelling and spacing.
  • Do not recolour the feather gold, add shadows, outline the letters or set the wordmark in another typeface.

The logo began as an AI-generated concept; the final wordmark is checked for spelling and spacing.

08 · Voice

Dignified, warm, precise.

We speak like a good tailor: calm, exact and proud of the work. We are proud of our heritage and never turn it into a costume.

We sayWe don't say
"Cut in Italian wool, finished by hand.""Luxury like never before!!!"
"Isiagu, tailored to fit you.""Exotic African-inspired vibes."
"Ships in 6 weeks. Pre-orders are open for 21 days.""Limited stock, hurry!" (when it isn't true)
Igbo and Italian words, used correctly and explained once.Generic "African" labels for Igbo dress.

Writing rules

  • Name the cloth, the cut and the finish. Leave out empty adjectives.
  • Short sentences. One idea per sentence.
  • Never invent scarcity, reviews or press. Never call an AI-generated image a photograph.
  • Prices in euros (EUR). Sizes S, M, L, XL, XXL.

How we make things

  • We read the trend from our own mood boards, design, and open a pre-order before producing.
  • We produce only when pre-orders reach the threshold, and only with a supplier whose quote passes our guardrails.
  • We write to suppliers in English and keep every request for quote, quote and purchase order on record.

10 · Imagery

How our images look.

  • Editorial and natural: warm daylight or golden evening light, a 35 mm film feel, real fabric texture.
  • Places that join our two worlds: Milanese courtyards, Roman apartments, modernist buildings in Enugu, palazzo hallways, ceremony courtyards.
  • Adult Igbo men shown with dignity and confidence, never as exotic subjects.
  • No logos of other brands, no text burnt into images, no watermarks.

About the images in this book

Every image in this edition, including the cover and the logo concept, is AI-generated inspiration made with ChatGPT image generation. They are mood-board material that sets our direction. They are not photographs of products, people or places, and they are labelled as AI-generated wherever we use them. Product photographs will replace them once the first garments are made.
